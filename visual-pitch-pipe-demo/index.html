Visual Pitch Pipe

A tuner you read instead of hear. I built it for my own choir rehearsals, where I kept needing my exact pitch from the sheet music in front of me.

Why I built it

I sing in a choir. In rehearsal the director gives a starting pitch, the section scatters, and I need my note, from the ink on my page, fast. I'd find my starting note on the staff and then guess. Hum around until it sounded right. It wasted time and I never trusted it.

I wanted the reverse of a tuner. A tuner listens to what you sing. I wanted something that reads what I see: this note, in this key, in my clef, just play it. Hence the name. A visual pitch pipe. You match the music with your eyes; it gives you the pitch.

How it works

  • Pick your clef. Treble, bass, alto, tenor. I sing wherever the section needs me, so all four are in.
  • Set the key signature. Up to 7 sharps or 7 flats, rendered on a real staff with VexFlow.
  • Slide the note. Move the note to the line or space that matches your music. Add a per-note accidental if the measure calls for one.
  • Play pitch. Web Audio plays the exact pitch. Underneath it's a small pipeline: clef plus staff position gives a note name, the key signature plus accidental adjusts it, and MIDI math turns it into a frequency.

Coming next: camera mode

Setting all of that by hand works, but it's slow when the music is already in your hands. The next version skips the form entirely: point your phone camera at your sheet music and tap the note. Same pitch engine underneath. The camera just replaces the typing.

  1. Looking for key signature. The app finds your staff in the frame, reads the sharps or flats at the start of the line, and locks in the key.
  2. Ready for note tap. Tap any note on the page. The app maps where you tapped to a line or space on the staff and works out the pitch.
  3. Playing note. It sounds your pitch, with a one-tap nudge if the read was off by a line.

Tap, hear, tap, hear. Eyes on the music the whole time.
